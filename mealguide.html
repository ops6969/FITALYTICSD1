Nutrition theory

Meal Guide

Learn how calorie surplus, calorie deficit, protein, fiber, and meal quality shape weight gain, fat loss, bulking, cutting, and maintenance.

01

Weight Gain

Weight gain usually happens when calorie intake is higher than the energy your body uses. Higher carbohydrate and fat intake can make it easier to create this surplus.

Carbohydrates are stored as glycogen in the muscles and liver. When overall intake stays consistently high, extra energy can also be stored as body fat.

This approach can help people who are underweight or intentionally doing a dirty bulk, but food quality still matters for health and training performance.

Common foods: dosa, waffles, french toast, chicken biryani, doughnuts, french fries, and pasta.

02

Weight Loss

Weight loss usually happens when calorie intake is lower than energy output. Choosing better foods can make this easier even when meals still feel filling.

High-protein and high-fiber foods support fullness, muscle retention, digestion, and more stable energy during a weight loss phase.

Fat loss comes from a consistent calorie deficit over time, supported by movement, sleep, and balanced meals.

Common foods: eggs, fish, bread, salads, rice, and chicken.

03

Bulk

Bulking is a controlled calorie surplus focused on muscle gain. It works best with enough protein, strength training, and a moderate increase in calories.

The body uses stored energy and dietary nutrients to support muscle repair, growth, and strength progression.

A cleaner bulk avoids excessive fat gain by keeping the surplus reasonable instead of pushing calories too high.

Common foods: beef, chicken, rice, bread, wheat, vegetables, maida, and lentils.

04

Cut

Cutting is a calorie deficit phase designed to reduce fat while keeping as much muscle as possible.

Protein stays high during a cut to support muscle retention, recovery, and fullness while carbohydrates and fats are managed carefully.

The goal is a leaner physique with more visible muscle definition, achieved through consistent nutrition and training.

Common foods: chicken, fish, eggs, salads, rice, fruits, and vegetables.

05

Maintenance

Maintenance happens when calorie intake roughly matches calorie output, helping body weight stay mostly stable.

This can be used after a bulk, cut, weight gain phase, or weight loss phase to hold progress while building consistency.

If someone reaches 75 kg from 65 kg and wants to stay there, they would move from surplus calories to a maintenance intake that supports 75 kg.
